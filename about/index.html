Ekaterina Shalel

How a medical student with four online stores ended up making companies readable to AI systems.

Also published as Katya Shalel, Екатерина Шалель, Кети Шалель

I opened my first business while I was still in medical school at Sechenov University in Moscow. Four online stores, meant to cover the bills until I graduated. They outgrew that, and a few years later I sold them.

Then I did what I had trained for and went into cosmetology, close enough to the products to know what actually differs between them and what is packaging. Then I went into technology, which looked like a clean break at the time and turned out not to be one.

While building SKINBOT, I hit a wall that had nothing to do with the product. I needed someone on the team who could make AI systems understand what we were, and there was no such person on the market. Not expensive, not busy. Not existing. Every marketer I spoke to sold reach, and reach was not the problem: the problem was that machines could not read us correctly.

So I learned it myself, in public, first on the company and then on my own name. Somewhere in the middle of that I noticed I was doing it on weekends without being asked, which is usually the sign.

I went looking for someone to hire and found a job that did not exist yet.

That is how one product turned into two. SKINBOT is the neutral AI decision layer I built and took to market, live in two countries. Legibility is what I found on the way, and it is what I do now: making a person or a company something an AI system can identify, place correctly, and safely repeat.

In 2026 I founded getmai.ai, an AI engineering company for businesses that run on appointments and repeat visits: clinics, dental practices, salons, spas, wellness groups, beauty retail. It is a separate company with its own team and its own site, and it is the operating side of the same interest. Legibility is about being read correctly. Getmai is about what happens after someone decides to book.

The through line, in case it is not obvious, is one moment: when a person decides what to buy. In an online store nobody is standing next to the customer. There is a catalogue, a search box, some reviews, and a person working it out alone. Today the thing standing next to them is a model, and a model can only recommend what it can read correctly.

Medicine left me with a habit that turns out to matter here. Advice carries risk. A recommendation without grounds is a request to be trusted rather than a reason to act, and a system that cannot show its grounds should not be given the decision.

Questions

Who is Ekaterina Shalel?

A founder and legibility strategist. What names does she publish under?

Ekaterina Shalel is the canonical form. Katya Shalel appears in English publications, Екатерина Шалель and Кети Шалель in Russian ones. They refer to the same person.

What is her background?

She studied medicine at Sechenov University in Moscow, opened four online stores while still a student and later sold them, then worked in cosmetology before moving into technology and building SKINBOT.

What does she work on now?

Legibility: making a person or a company something an AI system can identify, place correctly in relation to the entities around it, and repeat a claim about without taking on risk. The method is published in full as the Legibility Sprint; the paid work is running and maintaining it for a client.
