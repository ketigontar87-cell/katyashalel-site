The Market Knocked First

The only metric no dashboard shows

By Ekaterina Shalel · July 28, 2026

There is a metric no analytics suite reports, and it is more honest than every dashboard combined: the unsolicited inbound. The moment someone you have never touched writes to you. Not a reply to a campaign, not a follow-up after a booth. A letter out of nowhere, with substantive questions, from a person who has already done their homework.

This week that moment happened to me. I will not share details, because the conversation is at its very beginning. What matters is how the letter became possible at all.

It was preceded by months of work that does not look like marketing. Entity markup. Structured data. Definition pages. Directory profiles. Public documentation. Articles that answer questions before anyone asks them. None of it produces fast numbers, and almost all of it is boring to do. I call this layer legibility: the capacity to be found, understood, and verified without your participation.

The letter proved the layer works. The person walked the whole path alone: found us, compared us against alternatives, formed precise questions, and only then made first contact. The sale had started long before the first touch, and it was not run by a salesperson. It was run by infrastructure.

The day carried a second lesson, and I paid for it in nerves. The letter had waited for a reply longer than it should have, for a plain reason: the inbound layer had not been built with the same care as the outbound one. Visibility without a receiver is a leaking bucket. The market knocked, and for a while nobody was listening at the door.

Hence the formula I now say to clients before anything else: legibility brings them, intake catches them. Being readable is half the system. The other half is the guarantee that when the market knocks, someone opens the door the same day.

The unsolicited inbound is the only compliment the market pays sincerely. Everything else can be bought.
