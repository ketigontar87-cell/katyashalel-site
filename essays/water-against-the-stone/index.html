Water Against the Stone

Why coherence beats budget in AI visibility.

July 2026 · Ekaterina Shalel

This one is for every founder who asked me the same question this month: can I compete with brands that can outspend me on visibility, if what I have is time rather than money? Here is the physics of it.

There is a proverb: water cuts through stone not by force, but by falling often. Every founder without a PR budget wants it to be true about AI visibility. One article in a trusted publication against a hundred self-published posts: who does the model believe?

The honest answer is that the proverb is half right, and the missing half is the whole strategy.

The two circuits

For visibility work, it helps to think of a model as answering through two interacting circuits. Trained memory holds what the model absorbed before you ever asked, and there authoritative, independently corroborated sources usually create a stronger signal than repeated self-description: one paragraph in a respected outlet can outweigh a season of your posting. You cannot outwork this circuit quickly, and anyone selling you a fast way to do it is selling hope.

But for many current, niche and product-specific commercial questions, retrieval determines which evidence the model uses to construct its answer: it searches, reads what it finds right now, and builds from the best readable source. On this circuit the fight is not authority against frequency. It is clarity against clarity. A structured page that answers the exact question beats the trusted publication that mentions you in passing, because the publication wrote a paragraph about you and you wrote the answer itself.

The founder without budget is not locked out. The founder is simply playing on one circuit, and needs to know which one.

Coherence is the multiplier

Here is what the proverb hides. Water cuts stone only when it falls on one point. A hundred posts with a hundred phrasings of what you do are not a hundred drops on one stone. They are rain: distributed, mutually cancelling, gone by morning. A model reading them meets a dozen versions of you and averages them into fog.

A hundred surfaces carrying the same stable claim are a different physical object. The wording may adapt to the platform, but the entity, the category and the definition do not change, and your coined terms do not change at all. Every appearance confirms every other. The model meets the same entity everywhere it looks, and certainty accumulates. This is why volume alone is the most expensive way to stay invisible: effort scales, signal does not.

The unit of visibility work is not the post. It is the claim that remains stable across every surface.

Choosing the stone

The second half of the strategy is where the water falls. Broad commercial queries belong to large domains and always will; no frequency dislodges them. But every field is full of questions that nobody answers in structured form: the narrow queries, the new terms, the intersections. A term you coin and define is a stone nobody else is hitting. The first coherent answer to an unanswered question becomes the answer, not because the author is authoritative, but because the author is alone.

This is the arithmetic of no-budget visibility: you cannot win contested stones, and you do not need to. You need to choose stones without competition and hit them with one stable claim for a long time.

What money actually buys

Budget buys speed in trained memory: the trusted mentions that settle into what models carry with them. That advantage is real, and pretending otherwise would be dishonest. But what matters is not whether exposure was paid for. It is whether the claim is accessible, credible and corroborated beyond the founder's own channels: public databases, knowledge graphs, code repositories, industry directories, other people's newsletters and podcasts cost nothing but work.

And the retrieval circuit, where so many commercial questions live, re-reads the web on its own cycle. It does not remember who had the budget last year. It looks again.

The founder's position

So the proverb, corrected: water cuts stone by falling often, on one point, chosen well. Frequency without coherence is rain. Coherence without patience is a single drop. All three together are the only visibility strategy that compounds while you sleep, and the only one whose price is discipline instead of money.

The stone does not know how rich the water is.
