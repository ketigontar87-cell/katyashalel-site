The questions people actually ask

If any of these sound familiar, this is the problem I work on.

Why does ChatGPT recommend my competitor instead of me?

Recognition is not selection. A system can know your company exists and still decide that someone else fits the question better.

Why does ChatGPT know us but rarely mention us?

The failure can sit in retrieval, category fit, evidence, corroboration, or the decision criteria implied by the prompt.

How do I get my company recommended by AI?

There is no legitimate switch that buys a recommendation. What can be measured is the chain from retrieval to understanding, evidence, comparison and selection.

Is this just SEO for ChatGPT?

No. Search visibility is one input. Recommendation is a decision problem.

Does my website change what AI says about me?

Sometimes. But changing pages before diagnosing the failure can make the signal noisier rather than stronger.

Do I need Wikipedia?

Not automatically. Authority is the evidence environment a system can retrieve and reconcile, not one magic page.

Being known is not the same as being chosen.

Most companies are still optimizing for recognition: can the system identify us, cite us, summarize us?

The harder question is selection. When a user asks for the best fit under real constraints, do you make the shortlist at all?

Selection asks: does the system choose you?

AI Legibility

What I mean by Legibility.

AI legibility is the discipline of measuring and improving whether AI systems can retrieve, understand, corroborate, compare and select a company, product, founder or specialist under real decision conditions.

AI Search and GEO, technical SEO, digital PR, entity and brand authority, and measurement each address a different part of that evidence chain. AI legibility is not a synonym for any one of them.

Read the canonical, versioned definition.

Start with the decision you want to be considered for

We start with a real customer question and agree what a useful outcome would look like. Being identified correctly, making a shortlist and being recommended are recorded separately.

Before changing anything, we establish a baseline. We save the prompts, answers, visible sources and test conditions. Languages, markets and AI products stay distinct; API results are labeled separately from consumer-interface observations.

Next, we compare what your business claims, what public evidence supports and what appeared in the answer. We look for specific gaps: an unclear identity, an unsupported claim, missing decision information or a broken next step. Each explanation is labeled by what the evidence can support.

We prioritize changes, agree who will make them and record what was done. A comparable follow-up test checks the result. When other conditions have changed, we say so. A changed answer alone does not tell us which intervention caused it.

Choosing the right scope

When is a one-time audit enough?

The scope should follow the decision at hand. A focused audit can help clarify a specific problem, prepare for a launch or establish whether further work is worthwhile. Implementation and a follow-up test need an agreed scope. Further checks make sense when important scenarios continue to matter and the team is ready to act on the findings.

What does ongoing work involve?

Checking whether important changes have held, investigating meaningful shifts and deciding what to do next. That can include correcting outdated facts, reviewing a changed source or competitor, and testing an approved intervention. The frequency should reflect the stakes and pace of change. Rewriting content every month is not the goal.

Can you guarantee that AI will recommend us?

No. We can define relevant tests, document what appears, improve evidence and access, and check the result. We cannot control every model, source or personalized answer. A recommendation in a test is also different from an inquiry or a sale; business outcomes need their own evidence.

How will we know whether a change helped?

We compare repeated observations before and after the change using the same core scenarios and as closely matched conditions as possible. We keep the change log, failed tests and limitations. Where feasible, unchanged comparison scenarios help us interpret broader shifts. A before-and-after difference is an observation; a causal claim needs stronger evidence.
